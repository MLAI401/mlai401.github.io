1.Knowledge-Based Agents & Propositional Logic

In Topics 01–04, agents searched through state spaces or resolved constraint graphs. A Knowledge-Based Agent maintains an internal representation of the world as a set of formal sentences, tells new percepts to its Knowledge Base (KB), and asks its inference engine what action to perform.

1.1The KB Agent Architecture

The central paradigm is the strict separation between Knowledge (domain facts and physics) and the Inference Engine (general-purpose reasoning algorithms). An agent interacts with its KB through two primary operations:

TELL(KB, sentence) · ASK(KB, query) → answer

1.2The Wumpus World Environment

The standard running problem for logical reasoning (AIMA §7.2) is a 4×4 grid of rooms containing bottomless pits (producing a Breeze in adjacent rooms), a smelly Wumpus monster (producing a Stench in adjacent rooms), and a heap of gold (producing a Glitter in the same room).

  • Propositional Symbols: Px,y (Pit at [x,y]), Wx,y (Wumpus at [x,y]), Bx,y (Breeze at [x,y]), Sx,y (Stench at [x,y]).
  • Physics Rule: B1,1 ⇔ (P1,2 ∨ P2,1).

1.3Propositional Syntax & Semantics

Sentences are constructed from atomic proposition symbols and five primary logical connectives: Negation (¬), Conjunction (∧), Disjunction (∨), Implication (⇒), and Biconditional (⇔).

P ⇒ Q ≡ ¬P ∨ Q · P ⇔ Q ≡ (P ⇒ Q) ∧ (Q ⇒ P)

1.4Logical Entailment (KB ⊨ α)

A sentence α is entailed by KB (written KB ⊧ α) if and only if α is true in every model (possible world assignment) where KB is true: M(KB) ⊆ M(α).

2.Propositional Logical Inference

2.1Truth Table Model Checking (TT-Entails)

The simplest inference procedure enumerates all 2n truth assignments over the n propositional symbols in the vocabulary. It verifies that whenever KB = True, α = True.

Time Complexity: O(2n) · Space Complexity: O(n) · Sound & Complete

2.2Conjunctive Normal Form (CNF)

Every propositional logic sentence can be converted to a conjunction of disjunctions of literals (a conjunction of clauses) in 4 systematic steps:

  1. Eliminate ⇔: Replace A ⇔ B with (A ⇒ B) ∧ (B ⇒ A).
  2. Eliminate ⇒: Replace A ⇒ B with ¬A ∨ B.
  3. Move ¬ inward (De Morgan): Replace ¬(A ∧ B) with ¬A ∨ ¬B, and ¬(A ∨ B) with ¬A ∧ ¬B.
  4. Distribute ∨ over ∧: Replace A ∨ (B ∧ C) with (A ∨ B) ∧ (A ∨ C).

2.3Proof by Refutation (PL-Resolution)

The Resolution Rule states: resolving (A ∨ B) with (¬B ∨ C) yields the resolvent (A ∨ C). To prove KB ⊧ α, we add ¬α to KB and repeatedly resolve clauses. Deriving the empty clause □ proves unsatisfiability (contradiction), verifying KB ⊧ α.

3.Horn Clauses & Chaining

3.1Definite & Horn Clauses

A Definite Clause is a disjunction of literals with exactly one positive literal: (P1 ∧ … ∧ Pk) ⇒ Q ≡ ¬P1 ∨ … ∨ ¬Pk ∨ Q. A Horn Clause has at most one positive literal.

3.2Forward Chaining (PL-FC-Entails)

A data-driven inference algorithm that maintains a count of unsatisfied premises for each rule. When all premises of a rule are satisfied by known facts, the rule fires and its conclusion is added to the agenda. Forward chaining runs in O(N) linear time in the size of the KB!

3.3Backward Chaining

A goal-driven recursive search that starts at the query goal Q, finds rules concluding Q, and recursively attempts to prove their premises from known facts.

4.First-Order Logic (FOL)

4.1Syntax: Objects, Predicates, Quantifiers

Propositional logic lacks expressiveness for general statements across domains. First-Order Logic adds Objects (constants, variables), Relations (predicates), Functions, and two Quantifiers:

  • Universal Quantifier (∀): ∀x King(x) ⇒ Person(x) ("All kings are persons").
  • Existential Quantifier (∃): ∃x Crown(x) ∧ OnHead(x, John) ("There exists a crown on John's head").

4.2Unification & Generalized Modus Ponens

Unification finds a variable substitution θ = {v1/t1, v2/t2} that makes two logical expressions identical: UNIFY(Knows(John, x), Knows(John, Jane)) = {x/Jane}.

5.Quantifying Uncertainty & Bayes' Rule

5.1Degrees of Belief & Joint Distributions

Probabilities quantify an agent's degree of belief given evidence. The Full Joint Probability Distribution assigns a probability to every possible world combination across all random variables, summing to 1.0.

5.2Bayes' Rule & Diagnostic Inference

P(Cause | Effect) = [P(Effect | Cause) · P(Cause)] / P(Effect) = α · P(Effect | Cause) · P(Cause)

Bayes' Rule enables diagnostic reasoning: converting causal likelihoods obtained from clinical trials (e.g. P(Symptom | Disease)) into diagnostic posteriors (P(Disease | Symptom)).

6.Bayesian Networks & Probabilistic Inference

6.1DAG Syntax & Factorization

A Bayesian Network is a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) where nodes represent random variables and directed edges represent direct probabilistic influence. The full joint distribution is factored as:

P(x1, …, xn) = ∏i=1n P(xi | Parents(Xi))

6.2Markov Blanket & D-Separation

A variable X is conditionally independent of all other variables in the network given its Markov Blanket:

MB(X) = Parents(X) ∪ Children(X) ∪ Coparents(X)

6.3Exact Inference by Variable Elimination

Dynamic programming algorithm that eliminates hidden variables one by one through factor pointwise products (f1 × f2) and marginalization sums (∑Y f).

6.4Approximate Monte Carlo Sampling

  • Rejection Sampling: Draws samples from the prior distribution and rejects those inconsistent with evidence e.
  • Likelihood Weighting: Fixes evidence variables and weights each sample by the likelihood w = ∏ P(ej | parents). Never discards samples!
  • Gibbs Sampling (MCMC): Generates a sequence of states by updating one variable at a time conditioned on its Markov Blanket.

7.Practice Exercises with Worked Solutions

Exercise 1

Wumpus World Propositional Formulation & Model Checking

An agent starts at [1,1], senses no breeze (¬B1,1), and moves right to [2,1] where it senses a breeze (B2,1). The physics rule is B2,1 ⇔ (P1,1 ∨ P2,2 ∨ P3,1).

  1. Write the physics biconditional as equivalent CNF clauses.
  2. Given the KB containing ¬P1,1, ¬B1,1 ⇔ (¬P1,2 ∧ ¬P2,1), and B2,1, how many possible models exist over symbols {P1,2, P2,1, P2,2, P3,1}?
  3. Prove that P1,2 = False and (P2,2 ∨ P3,1) = True.
1. CNF Clauses for B2,1 ⇔ (P1,1 ∨ P2,2 ∨ P3,1) 
C1: ¬B2,1 ∨ P1,1 ∨ P2,2 ∨ P3,1
C2: B2,1 ∨ ¬P1,1
C3: B2,1 ∨ ¬P2,2
C4: B2,1 ∨ ¬P3,1
2 & 3. Model Checking & Entailment

There are 24 = 16 total models. ¬B1,1 forces P1,2 = False and P2,1 = False in all models where KB is true. Since B2,1 = True and P1,1 = False, rule C1 forces (P2,2 ∨ P3,1) = True in all satisfying models. Hence KB ⊧ ¬P1,2 and KB ⊧ (P2,2 ∨ P3,1).

Exercise 2

CNF Conversion & PL-Resolution Refutation Proof

Consider the knowledge base containing (A ∨ B) ⇒ (C ∧ D) and known premise A. Use the resolution refutation algorithm to prove C.

1. Conversion of KB to CNF 
(A ∨ B) ⇒ (C ∧ D) ≡ ¬(A ∨ B) ∨ (C ∧ D)
                   ≡ (¬A ∧ ¬B) ∨ (C ∧ D)
                   ≡ (¬A ∨ C) ∧ (¬A ∨ D) ∧ (¬B ∨ C) ∧ (¬B ∨ D)

Initial Clause Pool with Premise A and Negated Query ¬C:
C1: ¬A ∨ C
C2: ¬A ∨ D
C3: ¬B ∨ C
C4: ¬B ∨ D
C5: A
C6: ¬C  (Negated Query)
2. Resolution Steps 
Step 1: Resolve C1 (¬A ∨ C) with C5 (A)   → C7: C
Step 2: Resolve C7 (C) with C6 (¬C)         → C8: □ (Empty Clause / Contradiction)

Deriving □ proves that KB ∧ ¬C is unsatisfiable, hence KB ⊧ C is proven.

Exercise 3

Horn Clause Forward Chaining Trace

Given initial facts {A, B} and rules:

R1: A ∧ B ⇒ C · R2: C ∧ D ⇒ E · R3: B ⇒ D

Trace the execution of PL-FC-Entails to determine if E is entailed.

Execution Trace 
Initial count table: R1=2, R2=2, R3=1. Agenda = [A, B]. Inferred = {A:T, B:T}.

1. Pop A: Decrement R1 count → R1 count = 1.
2. Pop B: Decrement R1 count → R1 count = 0 (FIRES R1: add C to Agenda).
          Decrement R3 count → R3 count = 0 (FIRES R3: add D to Agenda).
   Agenda = [C, D]. Inferred = {A:T, B:T, C:T, D:T}.
3. Pop C: Decrement R2 count → R2 count = 1.
4. Pop D: Decrement R2 count → R2 count = 0 (FIRES R2: add E to Agenda).
   Agenda = [E]. Inferred = {A:T, B:T, C:T, D:T, E:T}.
5. Pop E: Goal query E reached! Return True.
Exercise 4

Bayes' Rule Rare Disease Diagnostic Calculation

Prior prevalence P(D) = 0.0005 (0.05% of population). Test sensitivity P(+ | D) = 0.99. False positive rate P(+ | ¬D) = 0.01. Calculate the exact posterior probability P(D | +).

Step-by-step Calculation 
Numerator for D = True:
  P(+ | D) · P(D) = 0.99 × 0.0005 = 0.000495

Numerator for D = False:
  P(+ | ¬D) · P(¬D) = 0.01 × 0.9995 = 0.009995

Total Evidence P(+):
  P(+) = 0.000495 + 0.009995 = 0.010490

Posterior P(D | +):
  P(D | +) = 0.000495 / 0.010490 ≈ 0.047187 (4.72%)

Key Insight: Despite a 99% accurate test, a positive patient has only a 4.72% chance of having the rare disease, because false positives from the 99.95% healthy population dominate.

Exercise 5

Exact Inference by Enumeration on Burglar Alarm

Calculate the exact numerical value of P(Burglary=True | JohnCalls=True, MaryCalls=True) on the AIMA Burglar Alarm network by summing over hidden variables Earthquake (E) and Alarm (A).

Enumeration Expansion 
P(b, j, m) = ∑e ∑a P(b)P(e)P(a|b,e)P(j|a)P(m|a)
           = P(b) ∑e P(e) ∑a P(a|b,e)P(j|a)P(m|a)

For b = True (P(B)=0.001):
  e=T (0.002): a=T: 0.95 × 0.90 × 0.70 = 0.5985
               a=F: 0.05 × 0.05 × 0.01 = 0.000025
               Sum_a = 0.598525 × 0.002 = 0.000001197
  e=F (0.998): a=T: 0.94 × 0.90 × 0.70 = 0.5922
               a=F: 0.06 × 0.05 × 0.01 = 0.000030
               Sum_a = 0.592230 × 0.998 = 0.591045
  Total Numerator(b=True) = 0.001 × (0.000001197 + 0.591045) = 0.000592

For b = False (P(¬B)=0.999):
  e=T (0.002): Sum_a = (0.29×0.63 + 0.71×0.0005) × 0.002 = 0.000366
  e=F (0.998): Sum_a = (0.001×0.63 + 0.999×0.0005) × 0.998 = 0.001127
  Total Numerator(b=False) = 0.999 × 0.001493 = 0.001491

Normalized: P(B | j, m) = 0.000592 / (0.000592 + 0.001491) = 0.2842 (28.4%)
Exercise 6

Likelihood Weighting Manual Simulation Step

Perform a manual Likelihood Weighting trace for 2 samples on the Burglar Alarm network with evidence JohnCalls=True, MaryCalls=True.

  • Sample 1 generated: Burglary=False, Earthquake=False, Alarm=False.
  • Sample 2 generated: Burglary=True, Earthquake=False, Alarm=True.

Compute weights w1 and w2 and the resulting 2-sample posterior estimate for P(Burglary=True | j, m).

Sample Weights 
Weight formula: w = P(JohnCalls=True | Alarm) × P(MaryCalls=True | Alarm)

Sample 1 (Alarm = False):
  w1 = P(j | ¬a) × P(m | ¬a) = 0.05 × 0.01 = 0.0005  (Burglary = False)

Sample 2 (Alarm = True):
  w2 = P(j | a) × P(m | a) = 0.90 × 0.70 = 0.6300      (Burglary = True)

Normalized Posterior Estimate:
  Total W(Burglary=True)  = 0.6300
  Total W(Burglary=False) = 0.0005
  Estimated P(Burglary=True) = 0.6300 / (0.6300 + 0.0005) ≈ 0.9992 (99.92%)

As N increases to 10,000 samples, the estimate converges to the exact ground truth of 28.4%.
